# Triangle center recipe data

For the next expansion stage, see the [full-range performance and dependency-graph plan](../../docs/triangle-centers-scaling-plan.md) (2026-11-01). It records profiling evidence, cached-source heading anomalies, and staged work on observability, caching, graph selection, and runtime construction costs; it does not change the recipes below.

The ten `chunks/*.txt` files contain 8200 defined centers (2026-11-01), including 239 ideal points with a direction and 19 without one. They are generated from 29 reviewed entries in `seeds.txt` and 8171 automatically extracted constructions from [ETC Parts 3](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart3.html), [4](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart4.html), [5](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart5.html), and [37](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart37.html). Git tracks the compact generated data and extraction rules. ETC pages are manually downloaded to `.cache/triangle-centers/`, which Git ignores; no runtime or scheduled synchronization occurs.

Each row is `id operation references...`. `M p q` means midpoint; `R p q` reflects X(p) in X(q); `C p` and `A p` are complement and anticomplement. `I p q` represents an ideal point by the direction of the finite line X(p)X(q). A bare `I` records an ideal point whose direction is unknown or unavailable; it is coverage metadata and cannot be used as a dependency in any construction. `J p q r s` intersects two lines; either line may contain one ideal endpoint, rendered as a parallel through its finite endpoint. `P p q r` projects X(p) onto line X(q)X(r); `L p q r` reflects X(p) across line X(q)X(r). `G p` and `T p` are isogonal and isotomic conjugates of X(p); an ideal input uses homogeneous barycentrics. `V p` inverts finite X(p) in the circumcircle of ABC using GeoGebra circle reflection. `F id:coefficient ...` stores an ETC combo of two to six finite centers; its result is the weighted sum divided by the sum of coefficients. Coefficients are checked formulas without spaces, paired with their references. Extraction accepts complete single-line sums using `X(n)` or `X[n]`, with two to six distinct references; ambiguous prose, products of points, and incomplete or conditional expressions are rejected. Shinagawa coefficients `(u,v)` on the Euler line are converted to the same `F` format as `(3u)X(2)+vX(4)`; `$...$` denotes a cyclic sum, and `and also` variants are separate candidates. Candidate coefficients must have matching scaling degrees. A zero coefficient sum gives an undefined finite point. Constant coefficients have the same selection risk as simple affine relations; variable coefficients rank below conjugates and above coordinate anchors. `B formula` stores one cyclic barycentric coordinate. The parser accepts explicit relations within the target X(n) entry, including `inverse-in-circumcircle of` and `circumcircle-inverse of`, equivalent-pair lists, wrapped intersections, symbolic `∩` intersections, and line lists that share a heading with curve incidences. Both `this line` and `these lines` headings may contain multiple pairs. Listed pairs may use braces, parentheses, or a dot typo between IDs; an isolated damaged numeric token is skipped. It reads up to ten eligible listed lines and proposes pairs of lines with disjoint endpoints. An explicit intersection is preferred over an incidence-only pair when its dependencies become available. Conditions and transformations relative to a triangle other than ABC are excluded.

Coordinate extraction accepts a direct first coordinate followed by `: :`, an exact cyclic `f(a,b,c) : f(b,c,a) : f(c,a,b), where f(a,b,c) = ...` pattern (also angle variables and function names g, h, or t), a single expression symmetric under swapping B and C, or three explicit coordinates whose normalized expressions agree under cyclic rotation, allowing commutative sum and product reordering. HTML superscripts, square brackets, wrapped formulas, implicit products such as `a2b4`, `aS`, and `rR`, arithmetic, constant powers, square roots including `Sqrt`, `π`/`Pi`, named trigonometric functions, `sinh`, `asinh`, and `atan` are normalized into a checked expression. A cyclic header or formula may continue across several HTML lines. Trailing author credits are removed before parsing, adjacent coordinate labels are recognized separately, and angle notation such as `sin 2A/3` is read as `sin(2A/3)`. A single source newline is treated as whitespace; blank lines and HTML block or `<br>` boundaries are retained. The importer and runtime have no fixed coordinate formula length, AST node count, or constant exponent magnitude limit; unsupported syntax is still rejected. Ambiguous `π8` is rejected rather than read as `8π`; X(3371)'s formula is a reviewed seed. Angle variables mean angles of ABC; lower-case sides mean opposite side lengths. The supported Conway symbols are `S=2 area`, `SA/SB/SC=(b²+c²−a²)/2` and cycles, `SW=(a²+b²+c²)/2`, `E=4R²`, `F=SW−4R²`, `s`, `sa/sb/sc`, `r`, and `R`. The Brocard angle ω is normalized as `atan(S/SW)`. One nested cyclic-function definition and two explicit local scalar definitions are expanded when their syntax is unambiguous; other symbols and more complex auxiliary-variable chains are deferred. Cyclic permutations generate the other two coordinates; a trilinear first coordinate is multiplied by `a` during import, so only `B` is stored or interpreted at runtime. A zero barycentric sum with a nonzero direction vector is classified as ideal and is not admitted through `B` yet; four parsed formulas had this result on both samples.

`V` candidates require a finite source away from the circumcenter on both sample triangles. The generated graph currently selects 59 such inversions. Long coordinate formulas and extended triangle-center expansions are admitted without a fixed size limit.

Run from the repository root with Python 3 and `beautifulsoup4`:

```sh
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py fetch --parts 1 2 3 4 5 37
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py native
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py build
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py check
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py verify
```

Before `native`, `build`, or `check`, run `/scripts/geogebra/browser/verification.html?page=center-anchors` against the local site, download its native coordinate sample, and copy it to `.cache/triangle-centers/native-atlas.json`. This ignored build-time sample covers two fixed triangle shapes and is never shipped to the app. Update the unavailable entries in `native-status.txt` from the same screen when the GeoGebra engine changes. The manually cached ETC Parts 1 and 2 feed `native`, which regenerates the direction entries in that same file from the target's own `(parallel)` or infinity-line incidence lists. This is an explicit maintenance action, not automatic ETC synchronization.

`fetch` is the manual page update step. `build` uses cached HTML and the build-time coordinate sample, writes compact chunks, and writes a local `selected.tsv` audit table with source sentences. `check` regenerates from the cache and compares with Git data. `verify` needs neither cached HTML nor the coordinate sample; it checks all chunk IDs, dependencies, directed cycles, formula syntax, ideal/finite input types, and exclusions. A candidate enters only when its dependencies are native or already constructible; forward numeric references are allowed. Selection first closes the relation-only graph without Shinagawa candidates, checks those candidates against available baseline points, coordinates, and listed lines, then forms weak components of the unresolved extended-center dependency graph; shared native and already closed points do not join components. By default, every screened coordinate anchor in an unresolved component is eligible. `--max-coordinate-anchors-per-component` optionally restores a per-component limit; previously selected IDs in `coordinate-priority.txt` remain eligible even when they exceed that allowance. A value of zero disables coordinate anchors. `seeds.txt` fixes the previously screened X(8004) line pair and includes X(3232) using the explicit cyclic barycentrics in ETC X(369), because native X(369) is unavailable; seed dependencies still have to close without cycles and pass the numeric graph screen; directionless `I` seeds are added only after graph selection, and cannot be inputs. Anchor selection favors unresolved dependents when a per-component limit is set. When an existing point is available, a combo must agree with it on every sample on which the combo is real and defined, with at least one valid sample; coordinate formulas must be homogeneous, and variants must agree on their common valid samples; formulas undefined on both samples remain eligible because their real domain may include other triangles; if a known ETC line is available on a valid sample, the point must lie on at least one such line. A missing line is not grounds for rejection. Input types are checked before choosing a recipe, so an unusable ideal input cannot displace a valid finite alternative. Reviewed seeds resolve three conflicting entries: X(5507) uses coordinates derived from its radical-center definition; X(8064) uses printed coordinates agreeing with the isogonal conjugate of the printed ideal coordinates of X(8063), although its listed line conflicts; X(72904) uses printed coordinates verified against multiple listed lines. After reopening the graph, simpler constructions replace formula anchors whenever possible. This batch retains 461 formula rows after selecting 590 candidate anchors. There is no default estimated construction length limit; `--max-length` can impose one explicitly. Isogonal/isotomic recipes bind their input point once before calling the shared conjugate builder, so a coordinate source does not get copied through every signed-area term. The full graph has maximum depth 6; the 8200 rows occupy 257,699 bytes before compression.

`native-status.txt` records 229 native IDs that GeoGebra returned as undefined on at least one of the acute and obtuse representative triangles. A bare ID means unavailable; an ID followed by two finite native IDs gives an ideal direction. Of the 229, 191 have a usable ideal direction from cached ETC Parts 1 and 2. X(1912) is marked ideal but its two available native lines disagree on the samples, so it remains excluded. The other 37 unavailable IDs are not assumed ideal. `seeds.txt` accepts explicit `id SKIP reason...` exclusions (and trailing `#` comments); unresolved dependencies are not permanent skips; X(72904) is now a reviewed coordinate seed. X(72060), previously excluded because its barycentric sum is zero, now has an `I` direction recipe. The build-time numeric graph screens selected constructions on two shapes. Coordinate formulas undefined on both are retained without numerical confirmation, since they may be defined on other triangles; non-coordinate constructions still need a valid sample. Known ideal directions are checked on both shapes; directionless ideal markers are appended only after graph selection and screening. A direct `TriangleCenter(...,idealId)` returns an undefined GeoGebra point because the engine has no drawable point at infinity; its direction remains available to dependent finite recipes. X(6346) remains excluded: its cyclic header uses `g` while the definition uses `f`, and the printed coordinates do not match the orthologic-center construction described at X(6344) on the sample triangles. The earlier 35-point real-engine smoke test remains available; this batch used rule tests and the numeric graph screen. These samples do not prove behavior on every special triangle. See the [staged plan](../../docs/triangle-centers-plan.md).

For an isolated source typo or damaged entry, review the exact ETC statement and independently check the intended construction or ideal-point status, then add the reviewed recipe and a source/reason comment to `seeds.txt`. Do not broaden a general parser rule solely to accommodate one damaged entry. For example, X(5959) has an `I` seed because its infinity-line list says `tthese`; its direction is not available from the current graph.


Import maintenance: `audit --parts ...` performs heading preflight before expensive extraction. Build/check also preflight their complete input set. Ambiguous or malformed headings currently fail with all detected issues; repair file-level source boundaries explicitly before importing those pages. Individual geometric exceptions remain reviewed seeds.

Build/check reuse gzip extraction checkpoints under `.cache/triangle-centers/extracted/`, invalidated by source, extraction code, filtering state, Part and parser version. Use `--no-cache` to bypass them. Formula caches are bounded and process-local. `--report .cache/triangle-centers/run.jsonl` appends stage/cache/closure events and five-second heartbeats with a run ID. Selection still recomputes globally; these caches do not freeze the graph across Part sets. See the scaling plan's implementation record for current limits.
