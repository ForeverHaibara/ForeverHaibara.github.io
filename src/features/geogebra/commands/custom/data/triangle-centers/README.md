# Triangle center recipe data

The ten `chunks/*.txt` files contain 8121 defined centers (2026-11-01), including 239 ideal points with a direction and 18 without one. They are generated from 20 reviewed entries in `seeds.txt` and 8101 automatically extracted constructions from [ETC Parts 3](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart3.html), [4](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart4.html), [5](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart5.html), and [37](https://faculty.evansville.edu/ck6/encyclopedia/ETCPart37.html). Git tracks the compact generated data and extraction rules. ETC pages are manually downloaded to `.cache/triangle-centers/`, which Git ignores; no runtime or scheduled synchronization occurs.

Each row is `id operation references...`. `M p q` means midpoint; `R p q` reflects X(p) in X(q); `C p` and `A p` are complement and anticomplement. `I p q` represents an ideal point by the direction of the finite line X(p)X(q). A bare `I` records an ideal point whose direction is unknown or unavailable; it is coverage metadata and cannot be used as a dependency in any construction. `J p q r s` intersects two lines; either line may contain one ideal endpoint, rendered as a parallel through its finite endpoint. `P p q r` projects X(p) onto line X(q)X(r); `L p q r` reflects X(p) across line X(q)X(r). `G p` and `T p` are isogonal and isotomic conjugates of X(p); an ideal input uses homogeneous barycentrics. `V p` inverts finite X(p) in the circumcircle of ABC using GeoGebra circle reflection. `F id:coefficient ...` stores an ETC combo of two to six finite centers; its result is the weighted sum divided by the sum of coefficients. Coefficients are checked formulas without spaces, paired with their references. Extraction accepts complete single-line sums using `X(n)` or `X[n]`, with two to six distinct references; ambiguous prose, products of points, and incomplete or conditional expressions are rejected. A zero coefficient sum gives an undefined finite point. Constant coefficients have the same selection risk as simple affine relations; variable coefficients rank below conjugates and above coordinate anchors. `B formula` stores one cyclic barycentric coordinate. The parser accepts explicit relations within the target X(n) entry, including `inverse-in-circumcircle of` and `circumcircle-inverse of`, equivalent-pair lists, wrapped intersections, symbolic `∩` intersections, and line lists that share a heading with curve incidences. Both `this line` and `these lines` headings may contain multiple pairs. Listed pairs may use braces, parentheses, or a dot typo between IDs; an isolated damaged numeric token is skipped. It reads up to ten eligible listed lines and proposes pairs of lines with disjoint endpoints. An explicit intersection is preferred over an incidence-only pair when its dependencies become available. Conditions and transformations relative to a triangle other than ABC are excluded.

Coordinate extraction accepts a direct first coordinate followed by `: :`, an exact cyclic `f(a,b,c) : f(b,c,a) : f(c,a,b), where f(a,b,c) = ...` pattern (also angle variables and function names g, h, or t), a single expression symmetric under swapping B and C, or three explicit coordinates whose normalized expressions agree under cyclic rotation, allowing commutative sum and product reordering. HTML superscripts, square brackets, wrapped formulas, implicit products such as `a2b4`, `aS`, and `rR`, arithmetic, constant powers, square roots including `Sqrt`, `π`/`Pi`, named trigonometric functions, `sinh`, and `asinh` are normalized into a checked expression. A cyclic header or formula may continue across several HTML lines. Trailing author credits are removed before parsing, adjacent coordinate labels are recognized separately, and angle notation such as `sin 2A/3` is read as `sin(2A/3)`. A single source newline is treated as whitespace; blank lines and HTML block or `<br>` boundaries are retained. The importer limits normalized formulas to 500 characters, 1000 AST nodes, and constant exponents with absolute value at most 32. Ambiguous `π8` is rejected rather than read as `8π`; X(3371)'s formula is a reviewed seed. Angle variables mean angles of ABC; lower-case sides mean opposite side lengths. The supported Conway symbols are `S=2 area`, `SA/SB/SC=(b²+c²−a²)/2` and cycles, `SW=(a²+b²+c²)/2`, `s`, `sa/sb/sc`, `r`, and `R`. Other symbols and multi-line auxiliary-variable chains are deferred. Cyclic permutations generate the other two coordinates; a trilinear first coordinate is multiplied by `a` during import, so only `B` is stored or interpreted at runtime. A zero barycentric sum with a nonzero direction vector is classified as ideal and is not admitted through `B` yet; three parsed formulas had this result on both samples.

`V` candidates require a finite source away from the circumcenter on both sample triangles. The generated graph currently selects 58 such inversions. Runtime coordinate formulas accept the same 500-character limit as the importer.

Run from the repository root with Python 3 and `beautifulsoup4`:

```sh
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py fetch --parts 1 2 3 4 5 37
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py native
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py build
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py check
python scripts/geogebra/tools/triangle_center_import.py verify
```

Before `native`, `build`, or `check`, run `/scripts/geogebra/browser/verification.html?page=center-anchors` against the local site, download its native coordinate sample, and copy it to `.cache/triangle-centers/native-atlas.json`. This ignored build-time sample covers two fixed triangle shapes and is never shipped to the app. Update the unavailable entries in `native-status.txt` from the same screen when the GeoGebra engine changes. The manually cached ETC Parts 1 and 2 feed `native`, which regenerates the direction entries in that same file from the target's own `(parallel)` or infinity-line incidence lists. This is an explicit maintenance action, not automatic ETC synchronization.

`fetch` is the manual page update step. `build` uses cached HTML and the build-time coordinate sample, writes compact chunks, and writes a local `selected.tsv` audit table with source sentences. `check` regenerates from the cache and compares with Git data. `verify` needs neither cached HTML nor the coordinate sample; it checks all chunk IDs, dependencies, directed cycles, formula syntax, ideal/finite input types, and exclusions. A candidate enters only when its dependencies are native or already constructible; forward numeric references are allowed. Selection first closes the relation-only graph, then forms weak components of the unresolved extended-center dependency graph; shared native and already closed points do not join components. By default, every screened coordinate anchor in an unresolved component is eligible. `--max-coordinate-anchors-per-component` optionally restores a per-component limit; previously selected IDs in `coordinate-priority.txt` remain eligible even when they exceed that allowance. A value of zero disables coordinate anchors. `seeds.txt` fixes the previously screened X(8004) line pair and includes X(3232) using the explicit cyclic barycentrics in ETC X(369), because native X(369) is unavailable; seed dependencies still have to close without cycles and pass the numeric graph screen. Anchor selection favors unresolved dependents when a per-component limit is set. When an existing point is available, a combo must agree with it on both sample triangles; coordinate formulas must be finite on at least one sample, and variants must agree on their common valid samples; if a known ETC line is available on a valid sample, the point must lie on at least one such line. A missing line is not grounds for rejection. Input types are checked before choosing a recipe, so an unusable ideal input cannot displace a valid finite alternative. X(8064) remains unselected: its printed coordinate does not lie on the listed X(107)X(7152) on either sample, and X(8063) has no known direction. After reopening the graph, simpler constructions replace formula anchors whenever possible. This batch retains 404 formula rows after selecting 524 candidate anchors. The default estimated construction length limit is 10,000 characters. Isogonal/isotomic recipes bind their input point once before calling the shared conjugate builder, so a coordinate source does not get copied through every signed-area term. The full graph has maximum depth 6; the 8121 rows occupy 202,854 bytes before compression.

`native-status.txt` records 229 native IDs that GeoGebra returned as undefined on at least one of the acute and obtuse representative triangles. A bare ID means unavailable; an ID followed by two finite native IDs gives an ideal direction. Of the 229, 191 have a usable ideal direction from cached ETC Parts 1 and 2. X(1912) is marked ideal but its two available native lines disagree on the samples, so it remains excluded. The other 37 unavailable IDs are not assumed ideal. `deferred-centers.txt` excludes X(72904), whose currently closed dependencies yield no consistent line pair. X(72060), previously excluded because its barycentric sum is zero, now has an `I` direction recipe. The build-time numeric graph requires each selected finite construction to be defined on at least one of the two shapes; some formulas, such as square roots of cotangents, are undefined on the other shape. Known ideal directions are checked on both shapes; directionless ideal markers are appended only after graph selection and screening. A direct `TriangleCenter(...,idealId)` returns an undefined GeoGebra point because the engine has no drawable point at infinity; its direction remains available to dependent finite recipes. The earlier 35-point real-engine smoke test remains available; this batch used rule tests and the numeric graph screen. These samples do not prove behavior on every special triangle.
